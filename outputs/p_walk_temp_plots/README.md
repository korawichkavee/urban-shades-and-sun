# Walking Trip Rate vs Temperature Analysis

## Overview

These plots show how walking behavior varies with temperature, accounting for both:
1. **Trip suppression** - People taking fewer trips overall at extreme temperatures
2. **Mode choice** - People choosing not to walk when they do travel

Generated by: `scripts/travel_surveys/plot_p_walk_given_temp.py`

## Methodology

### Data Source
- Metro travel surveys from 25 cities/surveys (1988-2007)
- 785,353 trips from 179,080 person-days
- Trips annotated with UTCI (Universal Thermal Climate Index) temperature

### Calculation Method

1. **Person-day aggregation**: Grouped trips by person-day (unique survey-household-person-date combinations)
2. **Temperature binning**: Created 5°C temperature bins centered at -12.5°C, -7.5°C, ..., 52.5°C
3. **Rate calculation**: For each temperature bin:
   - Walk trips per person-day = (total walk trips in bin) / (number of person-days in bin)
   - Total trips per person-day = (total trips in bin) / (number of person-days in bin)
   - Walk share = (walk trips) / (total trips)

### Key Assumption

**Survey sampling is representative of the population** - We assume that people surveyed on days at temperature T represent the behavior of the general population at that temperature. This means:
- Survey days are not systematically different from non-survey days
- People surveyed are representative of the metro area population
- Seasonal effects are captured by the temperature variable

### Important Note: Rates vs. Probabilities

The metric "walk trips per person-day" is a **rate** (expected count), not a probability:
- It's λ(walk | temp) = E[# of walk trips | person-day at temperature T]
- Can be > 1 because people can take multiple walk trips per day
- Example: 2.08 walk trips/person-day at 17.5°C means the average person takes ~2 walk trips on a day at that temperature

True probabilities would be:
- p(take ≥1 walk trip | person-day, temp) - probability of taking at least one walk trip (≤ 1)
- p(walk | trip, temp) - probability a trip is walk (shown as "walk share" in plots)

## Key Findings

**Walk trip rates by temperature range:**
- Cold (<0°C): 1.87 walk trips/person-day (37.8% of all trips)
- Cool (0-15°C): 1.94 walk trips/person-day (41.6% of all trips)
- Mild (15-25°C): 2.08 walk trips/person-day (51.0% of all trips)
- Warm (>25°C): 2.16 walk trips/person-day (53.9% of all trips)

**Overall:** 2.00 walk trips/person-day, 4.46 total trips/person-day, 44.8% walk share

**Pattern:**
- Walk trip rate peaks at comfortable temperatures (15-30°C)
- Both trip suppression AND mode shift occur at temperature extremes
- The combined effect shows strongest walking activity at mild-to-warm temperatures

## Limitations

1. **No zero-trip normalization**: Person-days are only those where someone was surveyed and appeared in trip records. People who took zero trips may be underrepresented if they were surveyed but not recorded in trip files.

2. **Cross-sectional, not panel**: Most people (90%) were surveyed on only one day, so we're comparing different people at different temperatures, not the same people across temperatures.

3. **No demographic controls**: Analysis doesn't account for differences in age, income, car ownership, or other factors that might correlate with both temperature exposure and walking propensity.

4. **Metro area aggregation**: All trips within a metro area survey are treated equally, ignoring neighborhood-level walkability differences.

5. **Historical data**: Surveys span 1988-2007, so patterns may not reflect current behavior (e.g., changes in remote work, ride-sharing).

6. **Temporal confounding**: Temperature correlates with season, time of day, and day of week, which independently affect travel. We control for these only through their correlation with temperature.
