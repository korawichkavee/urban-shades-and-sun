## Temperature Measures Compared

All analyses compare three temperature indices:

1. **Wet Bulb Temperature (wbulb)** - Measures heat stress accounting for humidity
   - Valid range: -40°C to 50°C
2. **Dry Bulb Temperature (dbulb)** - Standard air temperature
   - Valid range: -40°C to 60°C
3. **UTCI Temperature (utci_C)** - Universal Thermal Climate Index - comprehensive thermal comfort measure that accounts for:
   - Air temperature
   - Wind speed
   - Humidity (via dewpoint)
   - Mean radiant temperature (approximated)
   - Valid range: -50°C to 60°C

**Data Quality Note:** Extreme outliers have been filtered from all temperature measures. See `UTCI_FIX_SUMMARY.md` for details. Approximately 96.4% of data retained after filtering.

## Data Sources

- **Cities**: 20 hot climate cities worldwide
- **Images**: Street-level imagery with annotated pedestrian locations
- **Weather Data**: ERA5 reanalysis data via Open-Meteo
- **UTCI Calculation**: thermofeel library with ERA5 inputs
- **Temporal Coverage**: Historical data with date ranges varying by city

## Methodology

- **LOESS Smoothing**: Non-parametric regression with frac=0.3 for overall plots, frac=0.5 for per-city
- **Confidence Intervals**: 95% CI via bootstrap (100 iterations)
- **Balanced Sampling**: Each city weighted equally in overall plots to prevent large cities from dominating
- **Filtering**: Only includes images with:
  - Valid shade annotations (for shade plots)
  - Detected people (for "with people" plots)
  - Sunny conditions (for "sunny" plots)

## Interpretation Guide

### Temperature Comparison

**Expected Relationships:**
- **Wet Bulb** closely tracks heat stress in humid environments
- **Dry Bulb** shows raw temperature independent of humidity
- **UTCI** provides most comprehensive thermal comfort assessment

**Questions to Explore:**
1. Which temperature measure shows strongest correlation with shade-seeking?
2. Does UTCI better predict pedestrian behavior than simple temperature?
3. How does the relationship differ between humid vs dry climates?

### Shade Seeking Behavior

Look for:
- **Temperature thresholds** where shade-seeking increases rapidly
- **Differences between cities** (climate adaptation)
- **Time of day effects** (solar angle, lunch hours, etc.)

### Pedestrian Activity

Compare:
- **Weather suppression**: At what thermal conditions do people avoid outdoor activity?
- **Diurnal patterns**: Morning vs afternoon activity regardless of weather
- **Sunny vs all conditions**: How much does sun/cloud cover affect activity independent of temperature?
